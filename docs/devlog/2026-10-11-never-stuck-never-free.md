# Never stuck, and never for free

*11 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-11-never-stuck-never-free.vi.md)*

At the river, the child helps the fisher build a fish trap: a row of stakes, with no gap wider than his. In an earlier play test, a child put the stakes side by side, the pile ran out before the row reached the float, and the task could not be finished. So we made the fisher bring more stakes whenever the pile was empty. In the next play test, a girl of six pressed the big button 31 times without one tap, the row reached the float, and she said: "I didn't need to think, just press." Can a task be one that a child never gets stuck in, and still one where the child must think?

## What we found

- **The math must be the way to win.** In a study of a math game for children of 7 to 11, children learned more when the math was the action that wins the game than when it was a quiz between the play, and they chose to play that version about seven times longer (Habgood and Ainsworth 2011). A row that presses alone can finish teaches nothing about the gaps.
- **A good helper marks what matters, and shows.** In a classic study, a tutor helped children of three to five build a hard puzzle. The help that worked had a few parts: point out what matters, show how to do a step, and keep the child from giving up, without doing the task for the child (Wood, Bruner, and Ross 1976).
- **New things with no help are a quiet answer.** Each time the fisher brought stakes and said nothing, the child learned only that pressing works.

## What we decided

- **The help comes before the new stakes.** The pile has the stakes that the row needs at the gap of the fisher, and a few more. When a row that is too tight empties the pile, the fisher says "Hết cọc rồi. Có khe hẹp hơn khe của chú." and the narrow gaps glow. The child can take a stake back with a tap and put it again.
- **The task never becomes impossible.** If the pile is empty again, the fisher shows his gap on a short row next to the child, and then brings the stakes that the rest of the row needs. The record keeps the help, so the parent page does not count it as the child's own work.
- **A help happens where the child sees it.** In the same play test, a help at the bridge happened next to the fisher, 16 blocks away and off the screen. Every help now happens next to the child, in view.

## The limit of what we know

The studies were not about this game, and a child who runs out of stakes may feel stuck, as one child did at the bridge in the same test. We will watch in the next play test whether children fix the gaps after the marks, and how long it takes. The details are in issues #85 and #88.
