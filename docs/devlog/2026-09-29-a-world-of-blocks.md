# A world of blocks in the style of a folk print

*29 September 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-09-29-a-world-of-blocks.vi.md)*

The first build of Tre had flat pictures seen from above, and question screens. On the second day we changed it to a world of blocks, seen from above at an angle, and drawn like a Đông Hồ woodblock print. Why blocks, and why a print?

## What we found

- **In blocks, the math is visible.** A dike of 3 × 4 × 2 blocks is a volume, and a row of planks is a length. A square grid teaches coordinates, area, and perimeter, and children already know it from Minecraft.
- **The shape of the action must be the shape of the idea.** A board game with the numbers in a straight line taught number sense to children of four; the same game on a circle did not ([Siegler & Ramani 2009](https://siegler.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/sieg-ram09.pdf)).
- **The thing that a child counts must be plain.** Children in grades 4 and 6 who used realistic bills and coins solved fewer money problems ([McNeil et al. 2009](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ826505)).
- **Đông Hồ prints come from a village in Bắc Ninh,** near the places of the first chapters. They are printed on dó paper with natural colors, for example red from mountain gravel and black from burned bamboo ([Đông Hồ painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%C3%B4ng_H%E1%BB%93_painting)).

## What we decided

- **A world of blocks with a camera that turns in steps of 90°,** in the Đông Hồ style: flat colors from one palette, three flat tones for each color (top, left, right), no light model, ink lines only where they mean something, and a grain of paper over all.
- **Blocks where the child counts, curves where the world flows.** Full blocks for the ground, dikes, planks, and anything that the child puts or counts. Half blocks for houses and furniture. Quarter blocks for people, animals, and the small things in their hands. Smooth shapes for living and round things: tree crowns, bamboo, smoke, clouds, water.
- **The things that the child counts are plain.** The richness of the print goes on the houses and the trees, not on the plank or the bundle.
- **Everything is made in code from parts,** with a seed, not drawn. Tests can check the world, and nothing is baked: a felled tree takes its crown with it, and a house builds in one order and comes apart in the other.
- **All art is original.** We use the style of Đông Hồ, never a copy of a real print.
- **The world breathes.** When the child does nothing, the river flows, smoke rises from a kitchen at meal times, a buffalo snores in the shade, and a kingfisher comes one day in twenty. Nothing there teaches, counts, or rewards. It is there because the child looked.
