# Every hero starts small: why the game is called Tre

*28 September 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-09-28-every-hero-starts-small.vi.md)*

Before we wrote any code, we had to say what the game is for and who it is for. We wrote it on the first day, and it has not changed since. This entry tells why.

## What we found

- **Many heroes of Vietnam were ordinary people.** A boy who did not speak until he was three (Thánh Gióng), a woodcutter (Thạch Sanh), a fisher and diver (Yết Kiêu), a farmer (Lê Lợi), a mathematician from a village (Lương Thế Vinh).
- **Bamboo is the most ordinary plant of a Vietnamese village.** It grows fast, it bends in a storm, and it does not break. In the legend, when the iron weapon of Gióng broke in the battle, he fought on with bamboo ([Thánh Gióng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A1nh_Gi%C3%B3ng)).
- **Many Vietnamese children grow up outside Vietnam.** They hear the language at home, but they rarely meet the legends and the history in a form that they want to play.

These are not results of research. They are the reasons for our choices, and we say so here.

## What we decided

- **The name is Tre** (bamboo), and the line under it is "Every hero starts small." The hero starts as an ordinary village child with no special power, and grows with the mind and the body. On the title screen, a bamboo shoot grows one section for each era that the child completes.
- **The whole game is in Vietnamese and in English,** and a child can change the language. The names of people, places, and titles stay in Vietnamese with their marks in both languages (Thánh Gióng, Văn Miếu). The English mode shows a short meaning the first time.
- **The game is for children from Pre-K to grade 12.** The first release covers grades 1 to 5, and the first player is a boy of seven.
- **Thirteen chapters in the order of history,** from Lạc Long Quân and Âu Cơ to the scholars and healers of the 19th century. The story and the grade are separate: a child in grade 2 and a child in grade 10 can play the same chapter, and each gets problems at their own level.
- **A child needs no history before play.** The game teaches the history and the geography of each place and time as the child walks through it.

These choices come from what we want, not from a study. We write them down so that a later change is a choice too, and not an accident.
