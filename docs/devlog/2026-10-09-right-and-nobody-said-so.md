# Right, and nobody said so

*9 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-09-right-and-nobody-said-so.vi.md)*

The same two testers played again after the fixes of the last entry: Su, a girl of six who plays in Vietnamese, and Tí, a boy of seven who was born in the United States. This time they went farther. Su helped three of the five villagers, and Tí helped four. Tí was the first tester to get to the woodcutter. The count of the teacher was complete on the phone, and both children understood it: "Thừa một que, thầy để lại đống."

## What we found

- **When the work becomes right, a child must hear it.** Su put eleven rods on the mat. The teacher counted them, said that one rod was extra, and put it back on the heap. Now the mat had ten, and the work was right. But then the teacher showed a bundle of his own, and it ended with "Đủ mười que, thầy buộc được." Su thought that her bundle was done, and she added three more rods. At the healer the same thing happened: the extra bunch went back, the basket was right, and nobody said so. Su added more, and the basket was wrong again.
- **A help must do what it says.** The healer said "Bà bỏ giúp cháu mấy bó trước", which means "I put some in for you first". But the basket was already full, so she put nothing. Su: "Huh? I already put them all! Which ones?"
- **A child takes a line as an order.** To change the words, the healer said each fourth bunch as "Thêm một bó ngải cứu nữa" ("one more bunch of ngải cứu"). Tí heard "put one more", and he put a fifth bunch of each kind.
- **A line that says what happened can be lost.** When the smith said why the iron bent, a line of help came at the same moment and took its place. The children heard "go and see the fisherman", but not why.
- **Seeing your own work is part of the task.** At the woodcutter, Tí knew the plan: "For 3 same pieces, I mark after 3 sections." He stopped because he never saw his pieces side by side, and because the voice said "this piece is too short" without showing which piece.

## What we decided

- **After the world fixes the work, the person says the count of the work now** ("Giờ chiếu có mười que."), and no help comes while the work is right.
- **A help says only what it does.** A person who puts things for the child puts each one in view and counts it.
- **A line that says what happened is never cut or replaced.** The help comes after it.
- **The evening waits for the end of a task.** Both children stopped at night. Each task has a lamp, but the work was still hard to see. Now a task never goes into the night, and the person of the task stays at it.

Feedback tells a learner three things: where the learner is going, how the learner is doing, and what to do next (Hattie and Timperley 2007). "How the learner is doing" is also needed when the answer is right. A silence after a right result does not tell a child of six that the work is done.

## The limit of what we know

The testers were not real children, and the test machine ran the game faster than real time in some moments. Some problems came only from the test machine (a short push of the stick sent the hero far, and the game seemed to stop for some seconds), and we did not count them. The details are in issues #73 to #77.
