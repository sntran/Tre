# One button for small hands

*4 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-04-one-button-for-small-hands.vi.md)*

When we played the first trials ourselves, we did not know what to do. A tap on the rods on the mat took one rod back, but we wanted it to tie the bundle. A tap on the forge opened and closed the talk with the smith again and again. If a parent is lost, a child of six will be too.

## What we found

- **Small children need big targets.** Young children need touch targets of at least 2 cm × 2 cm, and a precise drag to a spot is hard for them ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/)). Children of 3 to 6 miss targets of 7 mm almost 30% of the time ([Vatavu et al. 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf)). On a phone, a counting rod in the world is only a few millimeters.
- **One rule is easier than many.** The five trials ended in five different ways: a tap on the rope, a tap on the iron, waiting for the tide, a tap on the healer, a tap on the woodcutter.
- **Show first.** Children who never saw a task need to see it done once, on a smaller example, before they try it ([Alfieri et al. 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ933606)).

## What we decided

- **A tap goes there; the button does it.** A tap on the screen only walks. One large action button does every step of every task on the thing in front of the hero: pick up, put down, take back, finish at the person, talk, and get on or off Nghé. The thing that the button will act on has an outline, and the button shows a picture of the act.
- **Space jumps and E acts,** as in the games that children already know (Minecraft and Roblox use Space for the jump).
- **Each person shows the work once on a small example,** and the next thing to touch glows if the child waits.
- **The work of each trial is in the open,** where no roof covers it.

We changed the controls three times in two days, each time after we played. The children who play Tre should never be the ones who find these problems.
