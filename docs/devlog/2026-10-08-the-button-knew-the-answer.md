# The button knew the answer

*8 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-08-the-button-knew-the-answer.vi.md)*

After a round of fixes, two new testers played the game as children. They did not know the game. They saw only the screen of a phone and heard only the voice, and a hidden log recorded what the game did at each touch. Su, a girl of six, played in Vietnamese. Tí, a boy of seven who was born in the United States, played in Vietnamese and then in English. In the first test of this kind, a child did two of the five trials of the village. Su did four. But at two of them, the big button did the math for her.

## What we found

- **A press that knows the answer takes the math away.** To help a child who only presses the big button, a press did "the next right thing". At the healer, it took herbs of one kind until the basket had enough of that kind, and then the next kind. At the fisher, it put each stake at the widest space that keeps the fish in. So presses alone filled the basket and built the row. Su: "I don't know how I did it! I just pressed." The child who tapped the stakes herself made gaps that were too wide, and the child who only pressed won.
- **A child cannot count what the child cannot hear or see.** After a wrong bundle or a wrong basket, the person was to count the child's own work aloud. In six wrong tries, the children heard their count zero times. Other help came first, and a second rule took the count away, because it read the taps of a lost child as checks of the work.
- **The work was in the dark.** A day of the game was eight minutes of play, so a child who rode the calf for three minutes came to the first task at dusk. The clock held the light while the child worked, and so the task stayed dark to its end. Tí, when the day came back: "Now in the day I can see everything!"
- **Help must show the next step.** The children waved for help 12 times. Five answers were "try first", to children who were already trying, and three were a nod with no words.

## What we decided

- **The button never knows the answer.** A press never reads a number that the child must find. It does the same again, and the taps and the counts of the child decide.
- **A wrong try is answered with the child's own count.** The world first shows and says what the child made, at each level of help. Then the person helps.
- **Night never hides the work.** The place of a task has its own lamp, the hero has a soft light edge, and the day is longer than the night.

## The limit of what we know

The testers were not real children. They read better than a child of six, and they could not hear the sounds of the game. The game moved more slowly on the test machine than on a phone, so the times of the play are not exact. The details are in issues #63 to #67.
