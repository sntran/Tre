# Show once, then step back

*5 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-05-show-once-then-step-back.vi.md)*

A child who never saw a task needs to see it done once. Our own play tests taught us that how a person shows it matters as much as whether the person shows it. Three of our mistakes taught us the rules in this entry.

## What we found

- **Show on another example, and never leave a part of the answer.** Guided doing with worked examples beats discovery with no help ([Alfieri et al. 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ933606)), but a help that ends with the answer to the same task takes the task away from the child. A shown example teaches only if the child watches it: the time that children spent on the last, full hint went with more learning ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)).
- **Other things on the screen take the attention away.** Interesting details that the task does not use lower what children remember ([Sundararajan & Adesope 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4)).
- **Our mistakes:**
  - Our own plan said that the teacher "puts one rod on the mat" to show the act. A child who heard "ten rods" and brought ten then had eleven on the mat, and the band snapped for a reason that the child did not cause.
  - When a child did well, the teacher made the task bigger in the middle of the round. The owner of the project tied three bundles, heard "one more time", tied three more, and nothing happened: twenty rods came onto the heap under his hands, and after four bundles 26 rods were still there.
  - In a picture from the owner's phone, while the planter showed her example, a house in front of her faded into a large dotted frame over half of the screen. She also talked about the market of the next hamlet over her example, and the head of the hamlet greeted the child at the same time, so two long lines showed at once.

## What we decided

- **Each person shows the work once, on a smaller example** next to the station, with the picture of each press of the button, before the child's first round. The numbers of the example are never the numbers of the child's round.
- **The example never touches the child's work.** When the person puts a thing down to show the act, the person takes it back ("Như thế này nhé."), so that the child's place is empty when the child starts.
- **The first try belongs to the child.**
- **A change of the task waits for the next round.** A person can make the task in the child's hands smaller, and says so, but never bigger.
- **When a person works, everything else steps back:** one person talks at a time; the person says only the words of the work, and small talk waits; and the view turns, in its steps of 90°, so that no house covers the work. These three are being built now (#38).
