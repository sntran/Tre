# Why a quiet hamlet, and not a market

*3 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-03-a-quiet-hamlet.vi.md)*

A father we know wanted his son of eight to learn the multiplication table faster. Our first idea was a market: people buy and sell, and multiplication is everywhere. We changed our minds before we built it.

## What we found

- **A market is crowded.** Many people and stalls make the paths and the movement difficult, and a child spends the time on walking around people, not on the math.
- **A paddy is open, flat, and quiet,** and its rows of seedlings are an array: the picture of multiplication.
- **One idea in many materials.** Dienes put one structure in different materials so that children "discover the common structure" ([Benedek on Dienes](https://real.mtak.hu/80683/1/Benedek_Paper_A4_format_v13_u.pdf)). One activity again and again does not teach multiplication; many things of daily life do.
- **Several related choices hold children longer.** Museum exhibits held visitors longer when they offered several related options and stations where people did not get in the way of each other ([Dancstep & Gutwill 2019](https://www.exploratorium.edu/sites/default/files/pdfs/DisruptingExhibitDesign_DancstepGutwill_FINALMay2019.pdf)).

## What we decided

- **Xóm Ruộng,** a small hamlet near Phù Đổng: three or four houses on stilts around an open yard, with a paddy, a duck pond, a stream, and a đình yard with a bronze drum. It is not a place of history. The stations are 8 to 12 cells apart on wide, straight paths.
- **Four activities, four pictures of multiplication,** each with another action:
  - the planting of a paddy: rows and columns (an array);
  - feeding the ducks: equal groups;
  - the fish traps: a missing factor;
  - the drum dance: skip counting in time.
- **Linked by the needs of the people,** not by a menu: the feast of the new rice (lễ cơm mới) needs eggs from the ducks, fish from the traps, and sheaves of the new rice; at dusk, the hamlet dances to the drum.
- **One memory of the facts for all four.** A fact that the child misses in the paddy comes back at the ducks, in another picture.
- **The parent sees one table of the facts,** ten by ten, in three states: exploring, getting there, confident.
