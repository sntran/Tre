# Right, and still a miss

*24 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-24-right-and-still-a-miss.vi.md)*

In this play test, six testers who did not know the game played as children of K to grade 5. A boy of grade 2 came to the river at night, where two young serpents swim up to the ford, and the slingshot throws rice balls to them. He counted the posts and pressed the big button that many times. The stone flew over. "The dragon was at post 2 and I pressed 2. I am getting upset." His count was right. This entry tells why a right count missed, and what else we found.

## What we found

- **A target that moves puts the clock back.** In the first two raids of each tool, an enemy stops at each post. From the third raid, it walks on while the child presses. A count of four takes about two seconds of presses; then the game waits more than two seconds for the last press, and the stone flies for about one second. In that time a serpent swims almost one post. We checked it with the logic of the game, without the screen: a child who counts the post where the enemy is and presses that many times missed 12 times in 12. Earlier this month we wrote "a count, not a clock": the count of a throw must not grow with the time that a button is held, because young children react slowly ([A count, not a clock](2026-10-07-a-count-not-a-clock.md)). A target that walks while the child counts brings the clock back by another door. To aim in front of a moving thing is a skill of its own: even children of about nine were less accurate and less consistent than adults when they pressed at the moment that a moving light reached a mark, at every speed, and the error grew with the speed (Figueiredo and Barreiros 2013). A raid that needs this skill measures timing, not number. Our design says that the concept is the only route to the goal. It must also be enough.
- **A win with no word.** The same boy hit the four scouts at the gate. All four pips went dark, and nothing came for about twenty seconds: "Nobody says anything! No yay. I am just standing here. Did I win?" The game ended a raid only when the last enemy was back at its start.
- **A demonstration that said "press".** At the boat, the fisher shows how to load a stake, and his line was "Bấm nút ở đống cọc: một cọc lên thuyền." ("Press the button at the heap: one stake goes onto the boat."). Two girls of grade 4 and grade 5 pressed along with him, and then could not tell their stakes from his: "Are those 2 mine or his?" A line of a demonstration must say what the person does, not what the child must do.
- **Two goals at one place.** A farmer who lost her ducks (an event of the day) stood by the pot of Gióng while the boy carried rice. A tap on her opened her talk, and from then on the help of the wave was about ducks: "But the top still says bring rice to Gióng! Which one??" With a duck in his hands, the help said "find a duck". He stopped.
- **The right color, said once.** At grade 5 the smith first does a fair test: two hot bars, one into the water, one onto a rack, then he bends both. The talk of the test took the place of the talk of the colors, so the right color was said one time, before the test. A girl of grade 5 had three bars that bent: "Why doesn't he just tell me what color he wants?" She also said that "the dark red and the right red look almost the same on the phone".
- **A thing with no picture.** A girl of grade 5 liked the fractions of the woodcutter ("1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12, so the kitchen gets 5/12") and could not see the bamboo that she had to mark. The stem was in the logic of the game, but its picture was missing: we had drawn stems of 6, 8, and 12 half blocks, and the levels of grades 3 to 5 use stems of 16 and 24. Our tests checked the logic and not the picture of each thing. She cut four times with her marks on the grass, and stopped.
- **Some results still had no word.** A blow of the bellows with too few lumps in the forge, and a tray of rice bowls in a pot that was not yet at ten, made only a small tap sound. "Is it in the pot or on the floor? I can't tell."

## Our test machine

The machine of the test drew between one picture every three seconds and about one picture each second. We know from the last play test that a slow machine makes some of what the testers see. This time the turn of the other children in the hopscotch (about three and a half seconds) passed between two pictures, and a boy of K wrote "they don't move". We did not count such things as problems of the game. The count that the voice cut at the boat, we did count: a line of what happened must not be cut by a later line on any phone.

## What we decided

- **A right count always hits.** At grades K to 3, the enemy nearest to the count stands still from the first press until the stone lands, in every raid. A walking enemy comes from grade 4, and only after the coach shows how to aim at the post in front of it. We added this to rule 1 of the design and to the raids.
- **The end of a raid comes at the last hit;** the enemies walk away after the line.
- **The person who demonstrates says what he does,** and a press of the child does nothing to the work until the turn of the child comes. We added this to rule 7.
- **The wave gives the help of the task that is open.** A person of another task near it waits with one short line and stands out of the way. We added this to rule 41.
- **Every thing that a task makes must have a picture,** and a test checks it for each level of each task.
- **The smith says the right color again** after the fair test and after each bar that bends, and the two reds must look clearly different on a phone.

## The limit of what we know

The study of timing measured children of about nine and adults, with a light that moved on a track, not children of seven with a slingshot on a phone. Younger children are likely less accurate, but we did not measure it. Also, our testers played as children; they are not children.
