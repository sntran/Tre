# Things belong to their time: barter, packed earth, and carts

*3 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-03-things-belong-to-their-time.vi.md)*

The first chapters of Tre take place in the time of the Hùng Kings and the legend of Thánh Gióng. An early version had coins at the market and brick paths in the village. Both looked normal. Both were wrong.

## What we found

- **Coins came more than a thousand years later.** The first coins of Vietnam were the Thái Bình Hưng Bảo of the Đinh dynasty (968–981), and even then, "for the next two centuries coins would remain a rarity in the daily lives of the common people, as bartering would remain the dominant means of exchange" ([Vietnamese cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_cash)).
- **Bricks came later too.** The brick tombs of the Red River region are from the Han time, after the time of the Hùng Kings.
- **Carts:** we found no evidence for or against carts in that time. We chose to keep them, and we say so here.

## What we decided

- **Era 1 trades by barter.** The household has a basket of goods: rice, fish, eggs, clay pots. At the market, the seller has a good and wants other goods at a rate: one for one (counting), two for one (doubles), or "two fish for five rice balls" (multiplication and ratio) at higher levels. Coins come with the Đinh, as a moment of the story.
- **Village paths are packed earth,** swept, with footprints. The brick pattern waits for a later era.
- **Carts stay** in the world.

This became a rule for all of Tre: things belong to their time. When the sources do not say, we decide openly, and we write the decision down.
