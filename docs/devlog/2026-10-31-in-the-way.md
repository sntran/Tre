# In the way

*31 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-31-in-the-way.vi.md)*

In this play test, seven children of K to grade 5 played after #140–#145. Most of what went wrong was not in the math. Something stood between the child and the thing to see: the big horse of Gióng on the cart, the body of the hero over the iron, the eave of a roof over the hero. Or the game moved something under the hand of the child: the view turned between two pushes of the stick, and the help of the elder showed a count that was not the answer. A child cannot learn from a thing that she does not see, and she cannot steer when the stick changes its meaning.

## What we found

- **The friend who goes with you stands on your work.** After #142, the grown Gióng rides with the child, as a friend and a guide. But his horse is the biggest figure in the game, and it stood on the cart in the mud, on the duck pen, on the mats of the share, and in front of the first post of a raid. A boy of grade 2 counted three posts where there were four, and missed: "Gióng's horse was hiding the first post before! That's not fair." We gave Nghé rules to keep out of the work (#94), but not Gióng. At the share there were two Gióngs, because the share still added the Gióng of #138.
- **The hero and the people hide the work too.** A girl of grade 1 at the quench: "I am standing on the iron, I can not see if it is red! My body is in the way." A boy of grade 3 at the ferry: "Everything hides behind the man." In the game, a house fades when it covers the hero or the work, but a figure never fades.
- **A turn of the view changes the stick.** The view turns by itself when the hero stands still under a roof (#111), and "still" was 0.4 seconds. A child who steers lifts the finger between two pushes, so the pause was a stop, the view turned, and the next push to the right took a girl of grade 4 back the way she came: "I rode right but now I am back at the plant garden." John Nesky, in his talk "50 Game Camera Mistakes" at GDC 2014, names a change of the controls at a cut of the camera as one of the mistakes. We wrote the same rule in our design long ago, and then let the game turn the view while the child steered.
- **Children copy the help.** In the first raid of the slingshot, the elder shows one throw at a post with no enemy, so that he does not give the answer (#93). A boy of grade 3 saw the elder count "một, hai, ba" while the scout stood at post 4, pressed three times, and missed: "So the hand was wrong??" Lyons, Young, and Keil (2007) showed that children of 3 to 5 copy the acts that an adult shows on a new thing, also the acts that do nothing, also after a warning. The child takes what the adult does as the way the thing works.

## What we decided

- **No figure stands between the camera and the work** (#146, #147). Gióng keeps out of the work and of the line to the hero, as Nghé does, keeps the pace of the hero, and is only one Gióng. The hero stands beside the work. A figure that still covers a thing that a line names goes pale and see-through. The stones of the cart lie in a row in the mud, where a child can count them.
- **The window opens when the outline shows** (#148): one test for "hidden", not three rays in the middle of the hero.
- **The view never turns while the child steers** (#149). A pause between two pushes is not a stop, the stick keeps its directions until the finger lifts, and the offer of a puzzle does not turn the view.
- **The elder shows on the real enemy** (#151): he says the post of the enemy, counts to it, and hits. A whole example on the real thing first, then the next one is for the child.
- **A button never shows an act that does nothing** (#150): the talk with a person of the road, the ride during a walk.

## The limit of what we know

The study of Lyons and his colleagues was with children of 3 to 5, and our boy was in grade 3; we saw only one child copy the count. Nesky speaks from the making of games for adults, not from a study with children. Our evidence is seven plays on a slow phone, where the voice and the frames sometimes came late. We will look in the next plays for the frames where a figure covers the work, for each turn of the view while a finger is on the stick, and for each miss just after a help.
