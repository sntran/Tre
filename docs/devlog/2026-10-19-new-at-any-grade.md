# New at any grade

*19 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-19-new-at-any-grade.vi.md)*

In the last play test, four testers who did not know the game played as children of K, grade 1, grade 2, and grade 4. The girl of grade 4 saw the math of the healer at once. Then she pressed the big button, took a bunch of leaves without knowing it, and after about fifty touches she left the task: "I'm grade 4 - the math was easy, the game buttons were the hard part." Nobody had shown her how to take and put a thing, because the game thought that a child of grade 4 did not need it. That rule was ours, and it was wrong. This entry tells five things that we got wrong, and what we changed.

## What we found

- **A child who is new to the game is new at any grade.** In "[The tasks grow with the grade](2026-10-18-the-tasks-grow-with-the-grade.md)" we wrote that older children get the first try. We made the first demonstration of a gesture fade for each child of grade 3 or more. But the grade tells the math of the child, not the gestures of Tre. The study that we used says the same: guidance that helps a novice can hinder a learner who knows more *about the task* (Kalyuga and others 2003). Each child who opens Tre for the first time is a novice at its gestures.
- **Three right answers are not mastery.** A boy of grade 2 answered three science questions in the practice of the teacher, each with three choices. After that, the game was 96 percent sure that he knew the states of matter, and the smith gave him the task of grade 3: read the color of hot iron, "đỏ anh đào". He did not know that word. The model of the game used a chance of a lucky guess of 0.2, but a child who chooses at random among three answers is right one time in three. With the right number, three right answers still give 85 percent, over the step of 0.8. A practice question is a weak piece of evidence, and a few of them must not change what a task asks.
- **The longest answer was often the right one.** The same boy said it: the longest answer was right for the bees, the ducks, the weather, and the hammer. We counted. Of 67 science questions with three text answers, the right answer is the longest one alone in 37 of them, that is 55 percent, where chance gives 33 percent. The guidelines for the writing of test questions say: keep the choices about the same length, and do not let the right choice stand out (Haladyna, Downing, and Rodriguez 2002, guidelines 24 and 28). Also, the game spoke an answer only after the child chose it, so a child who reads slowly chose by the shape of the text.
- **The voice dropped the goal.** At the teacher, the bubble "Thầy cần hai bó." showed, and in the same moment the first help started. The voice said only the help. The queue of the voice let a newer hint of a person take the place of an older line that still waited, and so the goal was never spoken. For a child of five or six, a line that the voice does not say was never said.
- **A box with no way back.** At the level of grade 4 of the teacher, the girl shares rods among desks, and the rest goes into a box. One rod went into the box by mistake. Nothing could take it out, so the desks could never be equal, and nothing said so. Our own rule says that a task never becomes impossible after a try.

## What we decided

- **The first demonstration fades only by what the child did in Tre.** A child who did a gesture in a task (carry, cut, quench, pour) gets only a point at the next task. The grade never removes a demonstration. A new form of a level (the band before a tie, the model basket, a rod into the box) gets its own first demonstration one time.
- **A task changes its level only on evidence from the world.** The level of a task moves only when its skill has three or more commits from tasks in the world. The answers of the practice still count, but alone they never move a task. A question with k choices counts with a chance of a lucky guess of at least 1 in k.
- **Questions that do not give away the answer.** The choices of a question have about the same length, the voice says each choice before the child chooses, and a practice has a fixed number of questions, with the number that is left in view.
- **The goal is always spoken.** A line that gives the goal of a task, or what is left to do, keeps its place in the queue of the voice, as a line of what happened does. A help that a child asks for with a wave is always spoken.
- **Each place that takes a thing gives it back.** A tap and a press take a thing back from a desk, a box, or a basket.

## The limit of what we know

The number of three commits is our choice, not a result from a study. The research tells us that a few right answers do not show mastery, and that the efficient first try in the world is the best signal (rule 19), but not how many tries a level needs. We will check in the next play tests that no child gets a level above the grade with words that the child does not know, and that a strong child still moves up.
