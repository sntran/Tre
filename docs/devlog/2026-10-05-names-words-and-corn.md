# Cô Năm, not cô Cấy: names, words, and corn

*5 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-05-names-words-and-corn.vi.md)*

In the multiplication hamlet of Xóm Ruộng, the first names of the people came from their work: cô Cấy (the planter), chị Vịt (the duck girl), chú Lưới, ông Trống. They sounded silly, and nobody in a Vietnamese village is called that way. In the villages, people call a person by a word of kinship and the order of birth in the family: cô Năm, chú Hai, cậu Tám.

## What we found

- **The order of birth is not the same everywhere.** In the north, the first child is Cả and the second is Hai. In the south, the first child is Hai ([Báo Cà Mau](https://baocamau.vn/tai-sao-nguoi-mien-nam-goi-anh-ca-la-anh-hai--a5375.html); [Báo Pháp Luật](https://baophapluat.vn/thu-vi-su-khac-biet-cua-gia-dinh-viet-cac-vung-mien-post221458.html)).
- **The southern way is old in the center.** Nguyễn Nhạc, the eldest of the three Tây Sơn brothers, was called "anh Hai Trầu" by the people of his place before 1771 ([Biên khảo](https://sites.google.com/site/bienkhao/t%E1%BA%A1i-sao-ng%C6%B0%E1%BB%9Di-mi%E1%BB%81n-nam-g%E1%BB%8Di-anh-c%E1%BA%A3-l%C3%A0-anh-hai)). When the custom began is not sure.
- **Everyday words change with the region.** In the center, "đâu" is "mô" and "gì" is "chi"; in the south, mẹ is "má" and bố is "ba" ([VietJack](https://vietjack.com/tai-lieu-tieng-viet/phan-biet-tu-ngu-toan-dan-va-tu-ngu-dia-phuong-pbtv.jsp)). Even numbers change: 101 is "một trăm linh một" in the north and "một trăm lẻ một" in the center and the south ([Vietnamese numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_numerals)).
- **We found corn in the wrong time.** Every house of Era 1 could have corn under the eaves. Maize came to Vietnam in 1597 by a legend, or between 1662 and 1723 by the scholar Lê Quý Đôn ([Thể thao & Văn hóa](https://thethaovanhoa.vn/chu-va-nghia-tu-ngo-den-bap-20251105052428822.htm)). That is more than 2,000 years too late for the Hùng Kings.

## What we decided

- **Names follow the region and the era.** The people of Xóm Ruộng, in the north, are Cô Năm, Chị Ba, Chú Tư, and Ông Cả. The same old man would be Ông Hai in a southern village of the Tây Sơn time.
- **People speak with the words of their region,** with a small note the first time a child meets a regional word ("mô = đâu").
- **The narrator, the screens, and the words of the math keep the words of the whole country,** as in the school books, so that a child learns one word for one number.
- **Things have their first year in the data, with a source.** No corn hangs under the eaves in Era 1; rice sheaves and gourds do.
