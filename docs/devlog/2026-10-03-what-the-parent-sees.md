# What the parent sees: does it work, and does the child like it?

*3 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-03-what-the-parent-sees.vi.md)*

Most learning apps tell a parent what the child finished and how many answers were right. We asked a harder question: how can a parent see whether the practice works, and whether the child likes it, is bored, or is stuck?

## What we found

- **A number from play can mislead.** An estimate from play puts children in order well, but it agreed only in part with a test on paper (r = 0.41 in Physics Playground; [Shute & Moore](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/ShuteMoore.pdf)). A "grade level" from the game would look more exact than it is.
- **Boredom is worse than frustration.** In three learning systems, boredom "was very persistent" and was "associated with poorer learning and problem behaviors, such as gaming the system"; frustration passed ([Baker et al. 2010](https://archium.ateneo.edu/discs-faculty-pubs/92/)).
- **Young children do not judge their own work well.** In elementary school, children get better at it with age, but problems stay ([Child Development 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13486)), and children of seven and eight stayed inaccurate even with feedback ([Zeitschrift für Entwicklungspsychologie und Pädagogische Psychologie, vol. 55](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1026/0049-8637/a000276)). A survey of the child would tell us little.

## What we decided

- **A learning log on the device,** next to the save: counts, times, and numbers only. Each day it is rolled up into small totals, and the events of the day are dropped.
- **Engagement comes from what the child does,** with no camera and no survey:
  - bored: fast, careless tries; long idle gaps; hopping between stations; sessions that get shorter;
  - frustrated: misses in a row; leaving a few seconds after a miss;
  - into it: near misses, faster recall of known facts, playing on after a set, coming back with no reminder.
- **A weekly note in plain words for each child,** the table of the facts in three states, and a line for each activity. The note also tells how the child checks the own work, and how much of the checking the people of the world still do. With small numbers, the note says "too few to tell yet".
- **No grade-level score, and no total score.**
- **Nothing leaves the device** unless a parent shares a summary, after the parent sees all of it.
- **At most one experiment at a time,** for example the gesture of prediction on or off. A child stays in one version, and the parent can see it and change it.

The log and a first view of it for the project are in the game now. The weekly note for parents is the next step (#25).
