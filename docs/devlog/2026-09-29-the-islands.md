# Hoàng Sa and Trường Sa: the facts in their place, and no verdict

*29 September 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-09-29-the-islands.vi.md)*

The country map of Tre must draw Hoàng Sa (the Paracel Islands) and Trường Sa (the Spratly Islands). More than one country claims them. A game cannot step around the question: a map that leaves them out says something too.

## What we found

- **Old Vietnamese records tell of the islands.** The scholar Lê Quý Đôn finished *Phủ biên tạp lục* by 1776, and it covers the Paracel and the Spratly Islands ([Phủ biên tạp lục](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%E1%BB%A7_bi%C3%AAn_t%E1%BA%A1p_l%E1%BB%A5c)). In 1836, by the order of Emperor Minh Mạng, Phạm Hữu Nhật took a crew of 50 to Hoàng Sa to measure the waters and set markers ([VOV](https://vov2.vov.vn/van-hoa-giai-tri/pham-huu-nhat-thu-linh-thuc-thi-chu-quyen-o-hoang-sa-29692.vov2)).
- **A tribunal gave an award in 2016.** On 12 July 2016, an arbitral tribunal gave its award in the case that the Philippines brought against China ([ASIL](https://www.asil.org/insights/volume/20/issue/17/south-china-sea-arbitration)). China says that the award "is null and void" and that China "neither accepts nor recognizes it" ([Ministry of Foreign Affairs of China](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng/zy/gb/202405/t20240531_11367334.html)). Vietnam was not a party to the case.
- **Children of many families play Tre,** Chinese-American families among them.

## What we decided

- **The map shows Hoàng Sa and Trường Sa as part of Vietnam,** with their Vietnamese names.
- **The game does not argue the dispute.** It shows what happened, in the place and the time where it happened, spread across chapters, quests, and side quests: the Hoàng Sa flotilla and the families of Lý Sơn in chapter 13; traders in a port such as Hội An whose maps do not agree; on Lý Sơn today, the ceremony for the soldiers of the Hoàng Sa flotilla (Lễ khao lề thế lính Hoàng Sa); old people who remember the battle of 1974 and Gạc Ma in 1988; a harbor office with the award of 2016, which notes that China rejects it and that Vietnam was not a party.
- **The facts come from people,** and a fact can come more than once, from people who see it in different ways: a Minh Hương family, a Hoa trader, a Cham elder, a Filipino fisherman, a European mapmaker. Each speaks from their own life and their own records.
- **No screen or notebook page collects the facts.** The game never sums them up, never asks the player for a conclusion, and never scores or rewards a view. What the player does with the facts is up to the player.
- **Each fact has a primary source,** and a historian checks every date, number, and quote before a release.

This is the most difficult rule of the design. We wrote it to be honest about what happened, and fair to every family that plays.
