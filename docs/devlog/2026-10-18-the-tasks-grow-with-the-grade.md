# The tasks grow with the grade

*18 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-18-the-tasks-grow-with-the-grade.vi.md)*

In the last play test, a tester who played as a girl of grade 4 put ten counting rods on the mat of the teacher three times, and four bunches of each herb into the basket of the healer. "That's for little kids in grade 1. I'm in grade 4!" At the woodcutter, she had to cut a stem of 12 rings into four equal pieces: "That's fractions - quarters! Finally something for grade 4." The five tasks of the village had only three levels, and grades 3, 4, and 5 all got the third one. We decided that each task gets a level for each grade.

## What we found

- **The grades ask for different math, not only bigger numbers.** In the Common Core, grade 3 is about multiplication and division within 100 and about unit fractions; grade 4 is about multi-digit multiplication, division with quotients of more digits, and equivalent fractions; grade 5 is about adding fractions with unlike denominators, decimals to hundredths, and volume. The Vietnamese program of 2018 has the same steps, with some more: in grade 4 a child computes the average of numbers (trung bình cộng) and solves problems through one unit (rút về đơn vị).
- **Vietnamese classrooms already have the right problems.** The "bài toán trồng cây" of grade 4 asks how many trees stand along a road with a given gap: with a tree at each end, the trees are the gaps and one more. That is exactly the row of stakes of the fisher.
- **Help that a beginner needs can get in the way later.** In a review of studies, guidance that helps a novice, such as a worked example, helped less or even hurt learners who already knew more (Kalyuga and others 2003). The same girl said: "The grown-ups always show me first 'Xem bà làm' - I wish I could skip that."

## What we decided

- **Each task has a level for each grade from 1 to 5.** The teacher asks for equal bundles of 3 to 9 rods in grade 3, a share of rods among desks with a rest in grade 4, and tenths and hundredths with bundles of 10 and 100 in grade 5. The healer asks for doses of a recipe, then for a basket made from a model, then for fractions of a basket. The fisher's row becomes a measure, then the problem of the trees, then a gap of one pole and a half. The woodcutter asks for one fourth, then three fourths, then one third and one fourth of the same stem. The smith's task moves from the color of hot iron to the heat that goes into the water, and then to a fair test.
- **The level follows the child.** It starts at the grade that the parent gives, and goes up or down one step with what the child shows, so a strong child of grade 3 can get the task of grade 4.
- **Older children count by groups, and the first try is theirs.** From grade 3, the people count by bundles or doses, not one by one, and they show a gesture only to a child who has not done it yet.

## The limit of what we know

The standards say what each grade learns, not how hard a game task feels. We will check in the next play test that a child of each grade solves most tasks on the first try (rule 15 asks for 80 to 90 percent), and not much more.
