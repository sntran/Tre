# Riddles in the evening

*18 October 2026 · [Tiếng Việt](2026-10-18-riddles-in-the-evening.vi.md)*

Vietnamese children grow up with riddles: câu đố in verse from their grandparents, câu đố mẹo that they ask each other at school, and the tales of clever scholars who solved the puzzles of a foreign envoy or a king. Should Tre have them? The first rule of the world says no question in the world, because a question with an answer field turns play into a quiz. We looked at what each kind of riddle does for a child, and found a way to keep all three without a quiz.

## What we found

- **Riddles with words of two meanings help children understand what they read.** In a small study, children of seven who read words well but understood little got seven sessions with jokes, riddles, and words of two meanings, and their reading comprehension improved more than that of a control group (Yuill 1998). A later study with third graders found the same kind of gain (Zipke and others 2008). For a child who grows up outside Vietnam, a riddle about the buffalo or the bamboo broom is also a way to learn the words of the village.
- **Trick questions catch children often, but there is no evidence that they teach.** Children of 5 to 12 gave the tempting wrong answer to questions such as "What do cows drink?" between 28 and 92 percent of the time, depending on the question (Young and others 2018). The study did not test whether practice makes children more careful, and we found no study that shows it.
- **Puzzles solved by doing are old and belong to many peoples.** The puzzle of the farmer who must take a wolf, a goat, and a cabbage across a river was written down in Europe more than a thousand years ago and is told in Africa and Asia too. Vietnamese tell of their own scholars, such as Lương Thế Vinh weighing an elephant with a boat.

## What we decided

- **Puzzles by doing are side quests.** A traveler at the ferry with a dog, a chicken, and a sack of rice; two pots at the well when the healer needs four bowls of water. The answer is always an action in the world, so the first rule stays. A tale about a real scholar waits for the era of that scholar, told as a folk tale.
- **Bà says a riddle in the evening, and the answer is a thing to find.** The child finds the thing in the village and taps it. There is no list to choose from, no mistake to count, and the riddle can wait for days in the notebook.
- **Trick questions are what the children of the village ask each other.** The hero only listens, the answer comes after a short wait, and they laugh together. It is fun, not a lesson.
- **Each riddle comes from a published collection,** and a person who knows Vietnamese folklore checks it, as we check every fact.

## The limit of what we know

The studies of riddles were small and in English. We do not know yet whether riddles in Vietnamese help a bilingual child in the same way. We will watch whether children look for the answers on their own, and which riddles they tell their parents.
