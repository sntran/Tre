# Learning by doing in games: the evidence and the rules for Tre

A research report, September 2026. The sources are linked in the text. A Vietnamese version is in `learning-by-doing.vi.md`.

Children aged 5 to 12 learn math and science best when they act inside a constrained world that shows the result of each action at once. They do not learn best from free play, and they do not learn best from drill. The numbers are clear. Unassisted discovery loses to direct instruction (**d = −0.38**), but guided discovery with feedback and worked examples wins (**d = +0.30**) ([Alfieri et al. 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ933606)). When the math is the game action itself, 7-to-8-year-olds scored **58%** on a delayed test, against **41%** for the same math as end-of-level quizzes, and older children chose to play the action version **seven times longer** ([Habgood & Ainsworth 2011](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/1/Habgood_Ainsworth_final.pdf)). Feedback that shows why an action failed (task plus process) has an effect of **d = 0.99**; a plain right-or-wrong mark has **d = 0.24 to 0.46** ([Wisniewski et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087/full)). Questions still help, but only as short, spaced, cued retrieval of material the child has already learned by doing. A hidden mastery model can read mastery from actions: in Physics Playground the in-game estimate had reliability **alpha = 0.87** and correlated **r = 0.41** with an external test ([Shute & Moore](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/ShuteMoore.pdf)). For Tre, this evidence supports the core decision: math and science are the input of actions in the world, never a question in the world. The evidence also sets limits. Young children need tight constraints and immediate feedback, rewards for finishing hurt them more than adults, and a physics engine without a goal that requires the concept teaches nothing.

## Guided doing wins; free play and long struggle do not

The theory behind "learning by doing" is older than games. Vygotsky put learning in the zone between what a child can do alone and what a child can do with help ([Vygotsky, Mind in Society](https://www.marxists.org/archive/vygotsky/works/mind/chap6.htm)). Montessori built materials that make an error visible to the child without an adult. Papert claimed that children learn best when they build things in a world where an error is a bug, not a shame. The evidence supports the structured versions of these claims and rejects the unstructured versions. Guidance during inquiry raised learning outcomes by **d = 0.50** across 72 studies ([Lazonder & Harmsen 2016](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1108443)). In a lottery-controlled Montessori study, **65%** of Montessori children chose to rework a hard puzzle, against **47%** of controls, and academic growth was steeper ([Lillard et al. 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01783/full)). Papert's strong claim did not survive: Pea and Kurland found no evidence that Logo transferred to general thinking skills ([Pea & Kurland](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED249930.pdf)).

The structure of the action must match the structure of the idea. This is the single most replicated finding for the youngest part of the age band. Siegler and Ramani gave 4-to-5-year-olds five sessions of 15 to 20 minutes with a linear number board. Number-line estimation linearity rose from **22% to 94%**. An identical circular board produced almost no gain (**11% to 26%**) ([Siegler & Ramani 2009](https://siegler.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/sieg-ram09.pdf)). The board did not teach. The linear shape taught, because it is the shape of the mental number line. Gesture studies show the same pattern for older children: third graders who moved their hands across both sides of an equation generalized to new problem forms, while children who only acted on the objects did not ([Frontiers 2021 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.650286/full)).

Struggle before instruction is age-sensitive. Across 53 studies, problem-solving before instruction had an average effect of **g = 0.36**, but the trend reversed for grades 2 to 5, where instruction-first won ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1308129)). One well-controlled study with 159 second-to-fourth graders shows the narrow version that does work: a short exploration of a novel problem, then instruction that refers to what the child tried ([DeCaro & Rittle-Johnson 2012](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ986177)). During that exploration, low-knowledge children learned more with feedback, and high-knowledge children learned more without it ([Fyfe & Rittle-Johnson 2012](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ994029)). Unguided recall collapses in this age band. Fourth graders recalled only **7% to 9%** of a science text with no cues, but **74% to 91%** with partial concept maps ([Karpicke et al. 2014](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2014/2014_Karpicke_etal_JARMAC.pdf)). The design default for grades 1 to 5 is therefore "generate within tight constraints," not an open sandbox.

## The world's feedback teaches when it shows why

Feedback is powerful on average and dangerous in detail. Across about 600 studies, feedback had a mean effect of **d = 0.41**, but about **one third** of interventions reduced performance. Feedback aimed at the person (praise, grades, comparison) caused the negative results; feedback aimed at the task and how to improve it caused the positive results ([Kluger & DeNisi 1996](https://www.winginstitute.org/does-feedback-improve-performance)). The 2020 update across 435 studies gives the information gradient: reinforcement alone **d = 0.24**, right-or-wrong plus the correct answer **d = 0.46**, and feedback that covers the task, the process and self-regulation **d = 0.99**. The channel (spoken, written, computer) did not matter ([Wisniewski et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087/full)).

This gradient settles the "bridge falls" versus "red X" question. A bridge that falls is outcome feedback. In information terms it is close to a red X. It becomes process feedback only when the world also shows why: which plank was short, where the load went, how far the arrow fell short. The main advantage of world feedback is what it does not do. It does not grade, praise or compare. Those are the self-level frames that produce the negative third.

Timing follows the learner, not the medium. Shute's guidelines give immediate feedback for difficult tasks and for low-achieving learners, as "a safety net so the learner does not get bogged down," and delayed feedback for simple tasks and for transfer. In computer-based instruction, immediate feedback had **d = 0.80** against **d = 0.35** for delayed. Shute also warns: avoid comparison with other learners, "use praise sparingly, if at all," and do not build hint ladders that always end in the answer ([Shute 2008](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)). For a 6-to-10-year-old on new content, the world must react within a second. For a child who already has the skill, a delayed or withheld result is acceptable.

Representations must fade. Elementary children who learned math equivalence with concrete objects first, then schematic pictures, then symbols, transferred better than children in concrete-only, abstract-only or abstract-first conditions ([Fyfe, McNeil & Borjas 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475214000942)). Perceptually rich objects hurt. Fourth and sixth graders who used realistic bills and coins solved **fewer** money problems than controls ([McNeil et al. 2009](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ826505)). The content-bearing object in a task must be plain: a plank with tick marks, a bowl, a row of coins. Rich Đông Hồ art belongs on the scenery, not on the quantity the child reasons with.

## Action games teach in under three hours; quiz wrappers do not

Every math game with a positive controlled study maps one operation to one gesture and delays notation. The doses are small. DragonBox 12+ produced **g = 0.24 to 0.27** in a randomized study of 1,850 seventh graders, the largest effect of the three tools tested; the immediate-feedback quiz tool was not significant after covariates ([Decker-Woodrow et al. 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/23328584231165919)). Slice Fractions gave 139 third graders **three hours** of cutting shapes; their gains were largest on abstract items without pictures, and they reached fourth-grade level ([UQAM](https://salledepresse.uqam.ca/communiques-de-presse/uqam-researchers-demonstrate-effectiveness-of-educational-video-game-slice-fractions-2/)). Wuzzit Trouble gave third graders **10 minutes, three times a week, for one month**, turning gears by integer amounts; the class improved **16.4%** on written tests ([Pope & Mangram 2015](https://families.brainquake.com/assets/pdf/Stanford-Pope_Mangram_SUMMARY.pdf)). Motion Math gave 122 fourth graders **five days of 20 minutes** of tilting a device to place a fraction on a number line; knowledge rose **over 15%** and self-efficacy about **10%** ([Riconscente 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1555412013496894)).

Zombie Division is the cleanest test of Tre's core decision. The same division facts were built into combat (strike a numbered skeleton with a weapon that is a divisor) or placed in quizzes between levels. Delayed test scores were **58%** for the action version, **41%** for the quiz version and **38%** for a no-math control. With free choice, children played the action version for **75.7 minutes** and the quiz version for **10.28 minutes** ([Habgood & Ainsworth 2011](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/1/Habgood_Ainsworth_final.pdf)). The authors define the rule: put the learning content in the parts of the game that are most fun, and build it into the structure of the world. The design order is content first, then mechanic, then story.

Physics games show the same rule and its failure case. Physics Playground lets students draw ramps, levers and pendulums to move a ball; 263 students gained significantly, and student choice of level order taught as well as adaptive sequencing ([Shute et al. 2021](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/JCAL2020.pdf)). Portal 2, a commercial game with no teaching intent, beat the brain-training app Lumosity on problem solving (**d = 0.59**), spatial skill (**d = 0.64**) and persistence (**d = 0.42**) ([Shute, Ventura & Ke 2015](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/portal1.pdf)). Angry Birds is the warning. Merely playing it "does not produce significant learning." Students learned about angle and distance only when given the goal to explain, not to win, and the belief that heavier objects fall faster survived in every condition ([de Aldama & Pozo 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0735633118823160)). A physics engine is not a physics lesson. If the child can win by feel, the concept is not learned.

The quiz-wrapper family fails on the same axis. Math Blaster was "purely a drill and practice" arcade game and the best-selling math title in history, with no independent efficacy record ([Wikipedia: Math Blaster!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math_Blaster!)). Prodigy's own commissioned evaluation found that a child needed about **888 answered questions per one-point gain** on a state test, that home play had no measurable effect, and that "students who consistently answered questions incorrectly were still able to move about the world" ([JHU key findings](https://marketing.prodigygame.com/Prodigy-Evaluation-Key-Findings.pdf)). A UK trial of drill apps with 376 children aged 5 to 6 found them "equally good to standard maths practice," not better ([Open University RCT](https://oro.open.ac.uk/82758)). By contrast, Zoombinis, which gives no instructions and lets wrong guesses become "the very evidence needed to solve the puzzle" ([Hancock & Osterweil](https://www.terc.edu/zoombinis/wp-content/uploads/sites/16/2021/01/ZoombinisAndTheArtOfMathematicalPlay.pdf)), showed that children with more play and more in-game computational-thinking practices scored highest on an external test across 45 classes ([TERC 2020](https://www.terc.edu/publications/the-development-of-students-computational-thinking-practices-in-elementary-and-middle-school-classes-using-the-learning-game-zoombinis/)). The dividing line is what the child thinks about. In Math Blaster the child thinks about the cannon. In Zoombinis the child thinks about the rule.

Game meta-analyses put the medium in its place. Games beat non-game instruction by **g = 0.33**, and a better-designed version of the same game beat the baseline version by **g = 0.34** ([Clark et al. 2016](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1090510)). Games worked better with multiple sessions, with added instruction, and in groups, and they did not raise motivation more than conventional teaching ([Wouters et al. 2013](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1008015)). Math games as a category showed only **d = 0.13** ([Tokac et al. 2019](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1214508)). Most published math games are drill in costume. The action games above sit well above that average.

## Questions belong with the teacher, spaced and cued

Retrieval practice and spacing are among the most replicated effects in learning science. In 50 classroom experiments with 5,374 students, **57%** showed medium or large benefits, across ages, subjects and test formats ([Agarwal et al. 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1319572)). Interleaved practice in 54 seventh-grade classes produced **61%** on a surprise test one month later, against **38%** for blocked practice (**d = 0.83**) ([Rohrer et al. 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1237752)). The optimal gap between reviews grows as the retention interval grows ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CPVWR2006.html)). These effects justify Tre's two question zones, practice with a teacher and the Văn Miếu exams, but they set conditions. For elementary children, retrieval must be cued, not blank ([Karpicke et al. 2014](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2014/2014_Karpicke_etal_JARMAC.pdf)). Questions must follow content the child has already learned by doing, not introduce it. Each question must end with corrective feedback. Multiple choice is not ruled out, but the child must not be able to guess without a consequence.

A doing-based world already contains retrieval. Each moment when the child must produce a learned procedure from memory to act (choose the divisor, set the plank sizes, split the loot) is a retrieval event. Spacing is a schedule, not a format. A later region that reuses an earlier skill, with a gap of days, is spaced review with no quiz.

## Rewards, praise and story decide whether the child tries again

The strongest evidence in the motivation literature is negative. Across 128 experiments, expected tangible rewards reduced later free-choice interest (**d = −0.36**), completion-contingent rewards more so (**d = −0.44**), and the effect was **greater for children than for college students**. Unexpected rewards had no effect, and informational verbal feedback about competence helped (**d = +0.66**) ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 2001](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)). A visible three-star bar where a child earns one star is a double penalty: a controlling reward and a public failure. Points, levels and leaderboards raised output volume in adults without raising intrinsic motivation or competence ([Mekler et al. 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563215301229)). What predicts the wish to play again is the feeling of competence and autonomy, not rewards or graphics ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf)).

Praise about ability harms. After "you're smart" praise, fifth graders chose easier tasks, solved fewer problems after a failure, and **38%** lied about their scores, against **13%** after process praise ([Mueller & Dweck 1998](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/Mueller_Dweck.pdf)). Failure must be cheap, private and informational. Celeste players persisted because respawn was instant and "only you can see how many times you died" ([IJHCS 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581923002082)).

Easier is safer than the theory says. The "85% rule" is a theoretical optimum for a specific class of learner ([Wilson et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12552-4)). In the field, 207 children in grades 3 to 6 who practiced at **90%** success attempted more problems and improved more than children at 75% or 60% ([Jansen et al. 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608012001951)). In over 20,000 Battleship Numberline sessions, the easiest games were the most motivating when difficulty was assigned; the inverted-U appeared only when players chose their difficulty, and a close loss held players almost as well as a big win ([Lomas et al. 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/313477156_Is_Difficulty_Overrated_The_Effects_of_Choice_Novelty_and_Suspense_on_Intrinsic_Motivation_in_Educational_Games)). Children also allocate attention to what is learnable: they persist with the toy on which they are improving ([Developmental Science 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/desc.70229)), and a tutor that picked exercises where each child's error rate was falling fastest beat hand-designed sequences ([Oudeyer, Gottlieb & Lopes 2016](https://www.pyoudeyer.com/oudeyerGottliebLopesPBR16.pdf)). Progress must be visible even in failure.

Story is two-sided. Interesting but irrelevant detail reliably reduces retention and transfer ([Sundararajan & Adesope 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4)). In the first Crystal Island experiment, the full narrative produced the smallest gain (**0.5 items**) and a plain slide deck the largest (**3.5 items**) ([McQuiggan et al. 2008](https://public.intellimedia.ncsu.edu/pubmgr/pubdb/pdfs/narrative-its-2008.pdf)). In the later version, where solving the mystery was the science, engagement and learning rose together ([Rowe et al. 2011](https://intellimedia.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/42/rowe-ijaied-2011.pdf)), and game fiction was a positive moderator in the gamification meta-analysis ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270)). Story that the child must use to progress is instruction. Story the child watches is load. For Tre, the legend must pose the problem, and the action must resolve the legend beat.

Two children on one device is an asset when each has a role. Joint media engagement research credits sibling co-play with "sociality, joint projects, and empowerment through sharing one's knowledge," and lists differentiation of roles and multiple planes of engagement as design principles ([Cooney Center 2011](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/12/jgc_coviewing_desktop.pdf)). Combining competition with collaboration was the most effective social arrangement in the gamification meta-analysis ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270)). Sessions end best when the device ends them at a natural stopping point; a two-minute parent warning made transitions rockier in children under 6 ([Hiniker et al. 2016](https://faculty.washington.edu/alexisr/ScreenTimeTantrums.pdf)).

## A hidden model can read mastery from actions

Stealth assessment is evidence-centered design applied to game logs. It has three parts: a competency model (the skills), an evidence model (rules that turn logged actions into observables, plus a probabilistic updater), and a task model (families of tasks tagged by skill and difficulty) ([Mislevy, Almond & Lukas 2003](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED483399.pdf)). Physics Playground used 74 Bayesian networks. Its evidence rules were simple. A silver trophy meant the level was solved. A gold trophy meant it was solved under par, with three or fewer drawn objects. Gold was the mastery signal. Across 168 students and four hours of play, gold-trophy performance had **alpha = 0.87** and the overall estimate correlated **r = 0.41** with an external physics test ([Shute & Moore](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/ShuteMoore.pdf)). Two lessons follow. Efficient success, not success, is the mastery signal. And a stealth estimate ranks players reliably but predicts a paper test only moderately, so Tre must not show parents a grade-level score.

Adaptivity itself did not raise learning. In the 263-student study, adaptive, linear and free-choice sequencing produced the same gains, while short physics animations were the most effective of eight supports ([Shute et al. 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1281101)). A small team must spend its effort on evidence rules and visual supports, not on the sequencing algorithm.

Bayesian Knowledge Tracing is a two-state model with four parameters per skill: prior, learn, guess and slip ([van de Sande](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1115329.pdf)). Its known problems are non-identifiability and degenerate fits. Standard practice bounds guess at **0.3** and slip at **0.1**, and declares mastery at **P(L) > 0.95** ([Gong, Beck & Heffernan](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~nth/pubs_and_grants/papers/2010/Journals/Gong%20How%20to%20Construct%20More.pdf)). A fit is degenerate if P(L) falls after a correct action, or if ten consecutive correct actions do not reach mastery ([Baker, Corbett & Aleven 2008](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/BCA2008W.pdf)). Deep Knowledge Tracing's advantage largely disappears when BKT gets forgetting, skill grouping and per-student ability ([Khajah, Lindsey & Mozer 2016](https://www.educationaldatamining.org/EDM2016/proceedings/paper_144.pdf)). Classical BKT with hand-set parameters and a forgetting term is a defensible choice for a browser game with no server.

Elo works for difficulty. Math Garden updates child ability and item difficulty after every answer, scores speed and accuracy together, and targets a **75%** success rate; it has served over 400,000 Dutch children ([Brinkhuis et al. 2018](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1187391.pdf)). Elo is "simple, robust, and effective" with performance comparable to more complex models ([Pelánek 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S036013151630080X)). But Elo calibrates item difficulty from crowds. A single offline device cannot. Tre must hand-set task difficulty by design parameters (gap length, number of plank sizes, group size) and update only the child's ability.

Luck must be separable from mastery. Students who game the system learn less; the signs are systematic guessing, rapid hint clicking and very short action latencies. In one tutor, students viewed **68%** of intermediate hints for less than one second ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)). Boredom and confusing hints precede gaming, and follow-up exercises on the gamed material fixed it ([LearnLab wiki](https://learnlab.org/wiki/index.php?title=Gaming_the_system)). Hints help most at medium skill; low- and high-skill learners learn more from unaided attempts. Time spent on a bottom-out hint correlates positively with learning, which means a demonstration only works if the child watches it ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)). In Physics Playground, worked-example videos were the favorite support, text-heavy facts were rejected, and first-draft hints failed for being too general ([Rahimi, Shute et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED612151.pdf)).

## Vietnamese village technology already holds the experiments

A century of hands-on materials gives one recipe. Vary one quality at a time. Build the error into the material so the child sees it without an adult. Let the child control the variable and repeat freely. Show the same idea in several objects so the child learns the structure, not the object. Place the object in a sequence toward the abstract. Montessori's "control of error" makes it "obvious at the sensorimotor level when the task has not been completed" ([Higher Ground](https://higherground.substack.com/p/friday-note-control-of-error)). Dienes's "multiple embodiment" puts one structure in different materials so children "discover the common structure" ([Benedek on Dienes](https://real.mtak.hu/80683/1/Benedek_Paper_A4_format_v13_u.pdf)). Cuisenaire rods worked when inside a curriculum and less well when spread alone; curriculum fidelity explained much of the variance ([Stanford Tizard summary](https://tizard.stanford.edu/2023/05/19/equational-reasoning-a-systematic-review-of-the-cuisenaire-gattegno-approach)). Manipulatives beat symbols alone, with the largest effect on retention and the effect moderated by instructional structure ([Carbonneau et al. 2013](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1007941)). Museum research adds the engagement side: exhibits held visitors longer when they offered multiple related options, multiple intriguing outcomes, and stations where several users did not interfere ([Dancstep & Gutwill 2019](https://www.exploratorium.edu/sites/default/files/pdfs/DisruptingExhibitDesign_DancstepGutwill_FINALMay2019.pdf)). Tinkering studios add immediate feedback, elements that are easy to assemble and take apart, a trivial first success, and ongoing complexification ([Petrich, Wilkinson & Bevan 2013](https://www.exploratorium.edu/sites/default/files/pdfs/PetrichWilkinsonBevan-2013-ItLooksLikeFun.pdf)).

Vietnamese village devices satisfy this recipe with little change. The gàu sòng hangs from a bamboo tripod by a rope tied "sao cho cân bằng lực," so one person steers a lever whose weight the rope carries ([Hànộimới](https://nhipsonghanoi.hanoimoi.vn/tat-nuoc-gau-dai-gau-song-624455.html)). The cọn nước turns because "lực đẩy của dòng nước chảy xiết" hits the blades, and bamboo tubes lift water and pour it into a channel at the top ([VietNamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/guong-nuoc-cong-trinh-thuy-loi-cua-nguoi-dan-vung-cao-xu-thanh-2422269.html)). The Tày water pounder fills a trough at one end of a beam; the water's weight lifts the pestle, the trough empties, and the pestle drops, "ngày đêm" ([Thể thao & Văn hóa](https://thethaovanhoa.vn/coi-gia-gao-nuoc-va-doi-song-cua-nguoi-tay-20140408152915750.htm)). The thuyền thúng uses about 25 bamboo poles and must be sealed with dầu rái or it fills ([Người Lao Động](https://tuoitre.vn/nld/thoi-su/ky-cong-nghe-dan-thuyen-thung-o-xu-quang-20190316161300743.htm)). The Cổ Loa arrowheads have three blades that make the arrow "quay như một mũi khoan," and a reconstruction fired five arrows at once with a tested range of 60 to 80 m ([Dân trí 2022](https://dantri.com.vn/doi-song/bat-mi-ve-suc-sat-thuong-cua-mui-ten-co-loa-va-no-than-an-duong-vuong-20220721092436589.htm)). Diều sáo flutes come in graduated sets of 3, 5 or 7, and the carved mouth sets the pitch ([Làng nghề Việt](https://langngheviet.com.vn/sao-dieu-lang-ba-duong-noi-truyen-thuyet-va-hien-tai-19818.html)). Traditional measures mix bases: 1 cân = 16 lạng, 1 yến = 10 cân, đấu about 1 L, thưng 2 L ([Vietnamese units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_units_of_measurement)). Ô ăn quan is a mancala game where the last stone's landing square decides a capture and 1 quan equals 10 dân ([Ô ăn quan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%94_%C4%83n_quan)). The lever appears in the đòn gánh, the gàu sòng, the cối đạp, the cối nước and the cầu khỉ. That is Dienes's multiple embodiment, already present in one village.

## The person who gives the task is a teacher who watches

A good tutor does not hand out a task and leave. The tutor watches, reads what went wrong, and changes the help or the task. This is the oldest finding in the study of tutoring, and it is the one that a game most often skips.

**Contingent help is the core.** Wood, Bruner and Ross named six functions of a tutor who helps a child build a puzzle: get the child interested, reduce the task to what the child can manage, keep the child on the goal, mark the features that matter, control frustration, and demonstrate ([Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.1976.tb00381.x)). The rule that makes it work is contingency: "If the child succeeds at this next level then next time offer less help. However if they fail, next time take more control." Children taught by this rule "were the most active and efficient and were able to perform the majority of the task alone" ([Wood, Wood & Middleton 1978](https://www.psy.gla.ac.uk/~steve/courses/archive/CERE09-10-safari-archive/LaurenClark/webarchive-index.html)). A decade of classroom studies names three marks of real scaffolding: contingency (help at or a little above the child's current level), fading, and transfer of responsibility. Contingency needs diagnosis first: "To provide contingent support, one must first determine the student's current level of competence" ([van de Pol, Volman & Beishuizen 2010](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/1766314/110506_05.pdf)).

**It works in software too.** Across studies, human tutoring had an effect of **d = 0.79**, and step-based tutoring systems, which react to each step and not only to the final answer, had **d = 0.76**: nearly the same ([VanLehn 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ946764)). Computer-based scaffolding in STEM had **g = 0.46** across 144 studies, and it stayed effective whatever the logic used to fade or add help ([Belland et al. 2017](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1133348)). The step is the unit: a game that reads each action, not only the result of a level, can tutor.

**The dilemma is how much.** Too much help and the child does not think; too little and the child flounders ([Koedinger & Aleven 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-007-9049-0)). For grades 2 to 5, a short try before the help works, and a long struggle does not ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1308129)). So the tutor waits for the first try, then helps at the lowest level that works, and gives less after each success.

**Boredom is worse than frustration.** In three learning systems, boredom "was very persistent", was "associated with poorer learning and problem behaviors, such as gaming the system", while frustration was "less persistent, less associated with poorer learning" and did not lead to gaming ([Baker et al. 2010](https://archium.ateneo.edu/discs-faculty-pubs/92/)). A tutor must notice a bored child as fast as a stuck one, and the answer to boredom is not more help but a new challenge or a new form.

**Facts are learned through strategies, then practiced.** Children master number facts in three phases: counting, reasoning strategies (using a known fact to find an unknown one), and mastery, the "efficient (fast and accurate) production of answers"; instruction fails when it jumps from counting to mastery without the strategies ([Baroody 2006](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ756752); synthesis in [Elmbrook 2016](https://www.elmbrookschools.org/uploaded/SSMigration/data/files/gallery/ContentGallery/Math_Fluency_Research_Synthesis.pdf)). Grades 3 to 5 with 10 to 15 minutes a day of strategy-based fact practice outperformed drill (Brendefur et al. 2015, in the same synthesis), and timed tests can cause math anxiety (Boaler 2014). For multiplication the strategies are the turned fact, doubles (×2, ×4 as double-double, ×8), fives and tens, nine as ten minus one group, and the split (7 × 6 as 5 × 6 and 2 × 6).

**Apprenticeship is the old form of all this.** In cognitive apprenticeship the master models, coaches, scaffolds, and fades, and the apprentice takes over more of the real work ([Collins, Brown & Holum 1991](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/COGNITIVE-APPRENTICESHIP:-MAKING-THINKING-VISIBLE-Collins-Brown/712a1b8a88040d80db28f2751288b5a45d702014)). A farmer who plants the first rows with the child and then leaves the rest to the child is this method in a Vietnamese village.

**Learning to learn is teachable, and it pays most in mathematics.** Training of self-regulated learning in primary and secondary schools had an average effect of **0.69** across 84 studies, with higher effects in mathematics than in reading or other subjects ([Dignath & Büttner 2008](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-008-9029-x)). In young children, metacognition interventions had **g = 0.48** at once and **g = 0.29** at follow-up, and they worked better for self-regulated learning when "delivered by teachers or task materials" than by researchers ([Eberhart, Ingendahl & Bryce 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-024-09405-x)). The cycle is plan, monitor, and reflect (Zimmerman 2002). For a game, the task material is the teacher: the task must make the child look before the commit, see why after it, and adjust, and the person of the task must hand the checking over to the child.

**But not as a separate, general skill.** The same literature sets limits. Domain-specific knowledge, not general skill, explains most of competence, and generic skills taught apart from content do not work ([Tricot & Sweller 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-013-9243-1)). Across cognitive training (chess, music, working memory, video games), "when placebo effects and publication bias are controlled for, the actual impact of cognitive-training programs on far-transfer measures is null" ([Sala et al. 2019](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/5/1/18/113004/Near-and-Far-Transfer-in-Cognitive-Training-A)). The EEF guidance on metacognition says that its strategies "are mostly applied in relation to specific content and tasks and are therefore best taught this way" ([EEF](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/metacognition/metacognitive-summary-of-recommendations_v.1.0.0_2025-11-10-154829_rjgg.pdf)). Young children's monitoring is weak: in elementary school it improves with age but difficulties persist ([Child Development, vol. 92, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13486)), 7- and 8-year-olds stayed inaccurate despite feedback ([Zeitschrift für Entwicklungspsychologie und Pädagogische Psychologie, vol. 55](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1026/0049-8637/a000276)), and a short stop-and-think window of a few seconds made kindergarten and second-grade judgments more accurate and less overconfident ([Wacker & Roebers 2022](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0274460)). Growth-mindset interventions had a very small effect on achievement, d = 0.08, larger only for low-income or at-risk students ([Sisk et al. 2018](https://artscimedia.case.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/141/2018/10/03145228/Sisk-et-al.-2018.pdf)). So the evidence supports teaching children to plan, check, and adjust inside each subject, with a pause to look, with the world as the judge, and with slow handover; it does not support one general learning skill that comes first and then helps with everything.

**For Tre:** the person who gives a task is a tutor with a body. The person waits for the first try, reads the error from the result and the timing, answers with one move in the world (show the action, mark what matters, make the task smaller, act out a strategy on another instance, change the picture, raise the challenge, or take the hard part), gives less help after each success and more after a miss, remembers what helped this child, and logs every move with its outcome, so that Tre can find out which moves work. Measurement is the weak point of scaffolding research ([van de Pol et al. 2010](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/1766314/110506_05.pdf)); a game that logs each move and the next commit can measure it.

## Conclusion

The evidence changes the question. The question is not "doing or answering." The question is "what does the child think about while playing." Every game with a positive result puts the concept in the child's hands as the only route to the goal, shows the consequence within a second, and keeps failure cheap. Every documented failure lets the child win without the concept, or grades the child instead of the action. Tre's core decision (math and science as the input of actions, questions only with a teacher and at the Văn Miếu) is the configuration the evidence supports, with one addition. The child must sometimes be asked to predict before acting, because prediction is what turned Angry Birds from a toy into a lesson.

The second change is about the mastery model. Its value is not adaptivity; Shute found none. Its value is the evidence rules. A model that scores efficient first-try success, discounts mashing, and applies a forgetting term gives Tre spaced review without a review screen and a parent report without a score. The scarce resource for a small open-source team is not the algorithm. It is the design of tasks where chance success is rare and the world's answer shows why.

## What Tre should not do

Do not put a question in the world. The quiz-between-levels version of Zombie Division learned less and was abandoned after ten minutes ([Habgood & Ainsworth 2011](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/1/Habgood_Ainsworth_final.pdf)).

Do not let the child win without the concept. Angry Birds by feel taught nothing ([de Aldama & Pozo 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0735633118823160)). Prodigy let children who answered wrongly keep moving ([JHU key findings](https://marketing.prodigygame.com/Prodigy-Evaluation-Key-Findings.pdf)).

Do not use stars, points or badges for finishing. Completion-contingent rewards reduce children's interest (**d = −0.44**), and children suffer more than adults ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 2001](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)).

Do not let a character praise ability. Ability praise made fifth graders quit sooner and lie about scores ([Mueller & Dweck 1998](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/Mueller_Dweck.pdf)).

Do not show leaderboards or attempt counts to others. Low positions cause competence frustration ([Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/intr/article/33/7/1/178330/How-leaderboard-positions-shape-our-motivation-the)), and comparison is a self-level frame that produces negative feedback effects ([Kluger & DeNisi 1996](https://www.winginstitute.org/does-feedback-improve-performance)).

Do not open with a long unguided struggle. For grades 2 to 5 the struggle-first effect reverses ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1308129)).

Do not build hint ladders that end in the answer, and do not allow instant skipping of a demonstration. Children clicked through **68%** of hints in under one second ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)); Shute warns against ladders that always terminate in the answer ([Shute 2008](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)).

Do not put rich art on the quantity the child reasons with. Realistic money caused more errors in grades 4 to 6 ([McNeil et al. 2009](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ826505)).

Do not add legend detail that the task does not use. Seductive details reduce retention ([Sundararajan & Adesope 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4)); the full-narrative Crystal Island learned least ([McQuiggan et al. 2008](https://public.intellimedia.ncsu.edu/pubmgr/pubdb/pdfs/narrative-its-2008.pdf)).

Do not borrow only the surface of a domain. Sokobond counts valence bonds but "bears no resemblance to actual chemistry" ([Gamecritics](https://gamecritics.com/sparky-clarkson/sokobond-review/)). Check that each device's behavior in the game is the real causal structure.

Do not show parents a grade-level score. A stealth estimate correlates only **r = 0.41** with an external test ([Shute & Moore](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/ShuteMoore.pdf)).

Do not rely on adaptive sequencing to raise learning. It did not ([Shute et al. 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1281101)).

Do not let the parent be the one who ends the session. Device-ended sessions at natural stopping points caused fewer tantrums ([Hiniker et al. 2016](https://faculty.washington.edu/alexisr/ScreenTimeTantrums.pdf)).

## Design guidance for Tre

### Numbered rules

1. Make the concept the only route to the goal. The task must be unsolvable without the operation. Evidence: Zombie Division (**58% vs 41%**) ([Habgood & Ainsworth 2011](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/1/Habgood_Ainsworth_final.pdf)); Angry Birds by feel taught nothing ([de Aldama & Pozo 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0735633118823160)).

2. Map one operation to one gesture. Drag a plank to compose length. Deal loot to divide. Turn a gear to multiply. Evidence: every math game with a positive controlled study does this ([Decker-Woodrow et al. 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/23328584231165919); [Pope & Mangram 2015](https://families.brainquake.com/assets/pdf/Stanford-Pope_Mangram_SUMMARY.pdf)).

3. Match the shape of the action to the shape of the idea. Lengths lie on a straight line with equal spacing. Groups are rows. Evidence: the linear board taught and the circular board did not ([Siegler & Ramani 2009](https://siegler.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/sieg-ram09.pdf)).

4. Show the result within one second, and show why. The bridge must show which plank fell short. The arrow must show where it landed. Evidence: process feedback **d = 0.99** against **d = 0.46** for right-or-wrong ([Wisniewski et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087/full)); immediate feedback **d = 0.80** against **0.35** ([Shute 2008](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)).

5. Let the world speak, never a grader. No "wrong," no red X, no praise, no comparison. Evidence: self-level feedback caused the negative third ([Kluger & DeNisi 1996](https://www.winginstitute.org/does-feedback-improve-performance)).

6. Ask the child to predict before the commit. The companion asks "Where will the arrow land? Point." Then the child fires. Evidence: epistemic goals turned Angry Birds into a lesson ([de Aldama & Pozo 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0735633118823160)); "Place Your Bets" pattern ([Nicky Case](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/)).

7. Give a free sandbox phase before the scored commit. Exploration is never scored as error. Only the committed action ("cross the bridge") is scored. Evidence: persisting in attempts is productive ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)); PhET students experimented more because nothing could break ([Wieman et al. 2008](https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Simulations_That_Enhance_Learning.pdf)).

8. Constrain tightly for grades 1 to 3. Few parts, one variable, a trivial first success. Open the walls with grade. Evidence: struggle-first reverses for grades 2 to 5 ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1308129)); unguided recall collapses at grade 4 ([Karpicke et al. 2014](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2014/2014_Karpicke_etal_JARMAC.pdf)).

9. Use a short explore-then-show cycle for new content. The child tries a novel task for a minute. Then the companion demonstrates, on a different instance, at counting pace. Then the child does the original. Evidence: explore-then-instruct worked in grades 2 to 4 ([DeCaro & Rittle-Johnson 2012](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ986177)); worked examples were the favorite support and animations the most effective ([Rahimi, Shute et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED612151.pdf); [Shute et al. 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1281101)).

10. Fade the representation inside each skill. Start with bowls and planks. Add tick marks and numerals beside them. End with numerals alone, with the old picture one tap away. Evidence: concreteness fading beat all other orders for transfer ([Fyfe, McNeil & Borjas 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475214000942)).

11. Keep the content object plain. Put Đông Hồ richness on the village, not on the plank, bowl or coin. Evidence: perceptually rich objects hurt ([McNeil et al. 2009](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ826505)).

12. Put the same concept in several devices. The lever lives in the đòn gánh, the gàu sòng, the cối đạp and the cầu khỉ. Evidence: multiple embodiment ([Benedek on Dienes](https://real.mtak.hu/80683/1/Benedek_Paper_A4_format_v13_u.pdf)); fluency from the same skill across many levels ([Shute & Moore](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/ShuteMoore.pdf)).

13. Introduce one new part per region. New device, new plank size, new rule, never two at once. Evidence: Baba Is You organizes levels by the word they introduce ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-baba-is-you-i-s-delightfully-innovative-rule-writing-system)); PhET's graduated complexity ([Wieman et al. 2008](https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Simulations_That_Enhance_Learning.pdf)).

14. Make failure cheap, private and instant. Undo is free. Retry is one tap and under two seconds. Attempt counts are hidden. The defeat message names what to change, never the child. Evidence: Celeste persistence study ([IJHCS 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581923002082)); Zoombinis treats wrong guesses as evidence ([Hancock & Osterweil](https://www.terc.edu/zoombinis/wp-content/uploads/sites/16/2021/01/ZoombinisAndTheArtOfMathematicalPlay.pdf)).

15. Target 80 to 90 percent first-try success on skill-bearing tasks. Let the child opt into a harder version for pride. Never force it. Evidence: 90% success gave the most practice and the most gain ([Jansen et al. 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608012001951)); the inverted-U appeared only with chosen difficulty ([Lomas et al. 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/313477156_Is_Difficulty_Overrated_The_Effects_of_Choice_Novelty_and_Suspense_on_Intrinsic_Motivation_in_Educational_Games)).

16. Reward with information and surprise, never with a contract. Competence feedback in words ("You used three planks. Last week you used five.") is safe. A surprise gift after the fact is safe. A star bar shown before the task is not. Evidence: informational feedback **d = +0.66**; expected tangible rewards **d = −0.36** ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 2001](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)).

17. Let the legend pose the problem. The story beat must need the action to resolve. Cut any legend detail the task does not use. Evidence: Crystal Island 2008 vs 2011 ([McQuiggan et al. 2008](https://public.intellimedia.ncsu.edu/pubmgr/pubdb/pdfs/narrative-its-2008.pdf); [Rowe et al. 2011](https://intellimedia.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/42/rowe-ijaied-2011.pdf)).

18. Give each of two players a role that the task needs. The older child makes the quantity decision. The younger child counts, sorts or taps. Hand the older child the hint to give. Evidence: differentiation of roles and "scaffolds to scaffold" ([Cooney Center 2011](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/12/jgc_coviewing_desktop.pdf)); cooperation plus competition ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270)).

19. Score efficient first-try success as the mastery signal. Record "solved" and "solved with minimum parts on first commit." Give the second most of the evidence weight. Evidence: gold trophy alpha **0.87** ([Shute & Moore](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/ShuteMoore.pdf)).

20. Design tasks so chance success is rare. Make targets that one obvious move cannot reach. Offer several sizes. Then set BKT guess low (0.05 to 0.15) and slip at or below 0.1. Evidence: standard bounds guess <= 0.3, slip <= 0.1 ([Gong, Beck & Heffernan](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~nth/pubs_and_grants/papers/2010/Journals/Gong%20How%20to%20Construct%20More.pdf)); guess and slip are the fragile parameters ([Baker, Corbett & Aleven 2008](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/BCA2008W.pdf)).

21. Hand-set BKT parameters and check them. Prior 0.1 to 0.4 for new skills, learn 0.1 to 0.3, mastery at P(L) > 0.95. P(L) must rise after every clean success. Ten clean successes must reach mastery. Do not fit from data. Evidence: degeneracy checks ([Baker, Corbett & Aleven 2008](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/BCA2008W.pdf)); mastery threshold ([Gong, Beck & Heffernan](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~nth/pubs_and_grants/papers/2010/Journals/Gong%20How%20to%20Construct%20More.pdf)).

22. Flag mashing and score it as no evidence. Signs: action latency too short for counting, monotone sweeps of sizes, no pause after a failure. Do not punish it. Simplify the task or have the companion demonstrate. Evidence: gaming detectors and the Scooter intervention ([LearnLab wiki](https://learnlab.org/wiki/index.php?title=Gaming_the_system)); the under-one-second hint criterion ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)).

23. Hand-set difficulty by design parameters and update only the child's ability with Elo. Gap length, number of sizes, group size and target distance define difficulty. Pick the next task about 1.1 to 1.4 logits below ability for 75 to 80 percent success. Evidence: Elo needs crowds to calibrate items ([Brinkhuis et al. 2018](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1187391.pdf)); Elo is simple and robust ([Pelánek 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S036013151630080X)).

24. Drive review from a forgetting term, not a review screen. Decay P(L) toward the prior each day. Grow the Leitner interval after each clean success (1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days). When a skill is due, raise its weight in the quest generator so the next region needs it. Evidence: forgetting closed most of the gap to deep models ([Khajah, Lindsey & Mozer 2016](https://www.educationaldatamining.org/EDM2016/proceedings/paper_144.pdf)); spacing gaps grow with retention interval ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CPVWR2006.html)). The interval constants are convention, not a cited result.

25. Trigger scaffolds just in time and fade them with P(L). Ladder: environmental cue, ghost preview, companion places one part, companion demonstrates on a different instance. Show levels three and four mainly in the middle P(L) band. Never end with the answer to the same instance. Evidence: hints help most at medium skill ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)); Shute's warning on hint ladders ([Shute 2008](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)).

26. Keep questions for the teacher and the Văn Miếu, cued and spaced. Ask only about skills with P(L) above 0.5. Give a partial cue for grades 1 to 3. Follow every answer with the correct answer and a one-line reason. Interleave skills. Evidence: guided retrieval in fourth graders ([Karpicke et al. 2014](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2014/2014_Karpicke_etal_JARMAC.pdf)); interleaving **d = 0.83** ([Rohrer et al. 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1237752)); classroom retrieval practice ([Agarwal et al. 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1319572)).

27. Report to parents per skill, in words, with an artifact. Three states: exploring, getting there, confident (P(L) below 0.5, 0.5 to 0.95, above 0.95). Show what the child built. Give the parent a lever to nudge the level. Offer the report in Vietnamese and English. Evidence: three-band status in Physics Playground ([Shute & Moore](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/ShuteMoore.pdf)) and Khan Academy Kids ([Khan Kids help](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041615571-How-does-the-learning-level-adjust-and-how-do-I-view-my-child-s-progress)).

28. End each session in the fiction, at a natural stop, by the device. One quest, one chapter, one raid. The sun sets over Cổ Loa. Tell the child the plan up front. Do not count down. Evidence: device-ended, routine sessions ([Hiniker et al. 2016](https://faculty.washington.edu/alexisr/ScreenTimeTantrums.pdf)). This sample is under 6; treat the rule as plausible for older children, not proven.

29. Plan for many short sessions with an adult nearby. The meta-analytic gains appear with multiple sessions and added instruction. Evidence: ([Wouters et al. 2013](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1008015)); 10 minutes three times a week was enough for Wuzzit Trouble ([Pope & Mangram 2015](https://families.brainquake.com/assets/pdf/Stanford-Pope_Mangram_SUMMARY.pdf)).

30. Build an explicit bridge from action to notation. In-game mastery is not paper mastery. DragonBox offers teacher materials for this transfer ([DragonBox Algebra Challenge](https://dragonbox.com/about/algebra-challenge)). Tre's teacher-practice zone is that bridge. Label the child's own actions with the symbol after the action is habitual.

### Proposed world tasks built on Vietnamese village technology

Each task follows one template. The legend poses a need. The child predicts. The child changes one thing and commits. The world shows the result and why. The child repeats freely. The model scores only the committed action.

A long pole overhangs and tips. The tick marks show the shortfall. | Solved; solved with minimum poles on first commit; number of resets; latency per placement; monotone sweep flag |
